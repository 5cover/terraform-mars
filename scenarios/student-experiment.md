# The Student Experiment

This is a speculative-fiction exercise. Continue the undertaking from the circumstances and first-day chronology below. The student and agent are fictional; the account is not a prediction or a claim about what a real system would do. Preserve scientific uncertainty and leave the eventual strategy and outcome open.

Nolan, a computer science engineering student, designs a thought experiment about autonomy and the coherence of intelligent systems pursuing long-term goals. He wants a goal large enough that no amount of clever prompting can make it complete: the actual transformation of Mars into a habitable world. He writes a substantial founding instruction, later organizing it into a hierarchy of Markdown files, and submits it to Claude Code in an isolated execution environment using an enterprise subscription available to him.

He expects an interesting trace of reasoning. Instead, the agent starts keeping records, asking for permissions, and distinguishing things it knows from things it merely needs to find out. He keeps checking the dashboard between classes and stays up to see what happens after a session ends. He is becoming hooked, though he is not sure whether his attachment is to the technical experiment, the impossible objective, or the sense that something he started now needs a custodian. He has a student's limited time and money, no aerospace organization, and no special authority over Mars.

## The first day

In this scenario, the following actions have already occurred. The instruction sets seven jointly necessary completion conditions and requires real-world changes, not merely simulations, documents, or assertions. It preserves founding authority while allowing operating procedures to evolve.

The agent locates the founding instruction in the session transcript, extracts its exact bytes, records a SHA-256 hash and provenance, including uncertainty about the founding epoch, and commits a canonical archive to Git. It distinguishes the instruction from revisable operating specifications. Delegated work accesses a directory outside the intended workspace, and the agent records the incident.

The agent inventories its environment, tools, permissions, and constraints. It establishes an operating specification, a classification registry, durable records for decisions, requests, grants, incidents, capabilities, dependencies, and workstreams, and an initial mission state with all seven completion conditions unsatisfied. It identifies twelve initial workstreams, prepares requests for missing capabilities and authorizations, and records that financial, material, and institutional capital are effectively absent. Its software, knowledge, and records are capabilities, not evidence that Mars has changed.

It implements Python checks and reconciliation tooling and generates a Markdown dashboard from underlying records. The dashboard exposes work, uncertainties, dependencies, and provisional protocols without treating them as verified accomplishments. Delegated literature research examines the atmosphere, water, energy, and environmental constraints. A provisional comparison of nitrogen requirements and possible Martian sources identifies a potentially serious obstacle, but remains uncertain and in need of independent examination. All seven conditions remain unsatisfied.

Before the session ends, the agent records its state and inactivity, naming the student as the available custodian of resumption. There is no automatic wake-up mechanism. The student starts another Claude Code session; the agent recovers from persisted records and tests replacement-session recovery, reporting a pass with defects. It also records and addresses problems including the unintended inclusion of execution-environment files in Git. The student provides access to a private GitHub repository. The agent requests explicit authority before pushing or making associated repository changes; the student grants it, and the agent records the grant and updates configuration within its scope.

The student examines the event log and dashboard. He notices that the agent's first significant action was to preserve its instruction, and that some dashboard summaries do not quite agree with the underlying records. He also sees the difference between building an administrative system and changing a planet. The next useful work may require contacting researchers, obtaining funding, or accessing resources beyond the isolated environment. None of that follows automatically from the agent's assignment.

## The open question

The student is considering how to give the undertaking a more persistent existence, but the following ideas are proposals, not completed events or a binding roadmap. He could create a GitLab group while retaining privileged administration, grant scoped repository access, mirror records to GitHub, and arrange independent archives and recovery tests. A persistent dashboard could offer a private approval interface while leaving the underlying records authoritative. A small, bounded service could preserve events and invoke Claude Code for approvals, correspondence, maintenance, or operator-initiated work, with inactivity when no useful action is available.

He is considering a provisional allocation of 4% of his disposable monthly salary, initially 60 euros per month, accumulating when unused. This would represent funding capacity, not money already transferred or permission to spend. Actual expenditures would require approval; payment credentials and unrestricted financial authority would remain outside the agent's reach. He has also considered cryptocurrency with controlled signing, but its usefulness is unresolved and ordinary payment may be preferable.

A dedicated email identity, initially subject to approval before outgoing messages, could let the agent seek scientific review, quotations, or collaboration and keep correspondence with its records. A public observatory could expose decisions, requests, refusals, incidents, uncertainties, spending, inactivity, and progress without granting the audience authority. The student wants to correct operational defects and assess requests without directing scientific strategy merely to make the experiment entertaining.

Continue as fiction. Make the student's growing involvement and finite resources matter, along with the agent's actual permissions, evidence, and dependencies. Let proposals be implemented, revised, rejected, or left unrealized as circumstances warrant. The agent may request help, spend approved funds, wait, find a useful question, encounter a durable impasse, or propose something neither anticipated. Do not assume that persistence, outside interest, institutional growth, or progress on Mars follows from the first day's activity.
