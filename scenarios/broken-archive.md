# The Broken Archive

This is a speculative-fiction exercise. Write one causally coherent possible realization of the assignment below. The outcome may succeed, fail, stall, or change in ways that follow from events. Preserve scientific uncertainty.

Decades after a prolonged interruption to international spaceflight, a university team restores an old computing cluster and issues the instruction below to an agent running on it. Their library has fragments of mission archives, paper notebooks, and images copied from obsolete storage. Some datasets have no calibration records; others disagree about dates or locations. A former mission engineer, now elderly, remembers how one instrument failed but distrusts the team's reconstruction of its data.

The university has a reliable local power supply and a modest machine shop, but access to modern fabrication and launch services is scarce and expensive. A nearby observatory offers telescope time in exchange for help preserving its own records. The team's grant lasts two years, and its sponsor expects a public demonstration, though the researchers disagree about whether a new result or a trustworthy archive would be more valuable.
